## Where the rebuttal pushed back (and the adjudicator agreed)

- **M1 — the *qualitative* conclusion survives.** Although "0.08" was unsourced and the notebook contradicted it, the small-effect conclusion holds under *any* defensible weighting (weighted *h* ≈ 0.07, weighted RD ≈ 3 pp). The fix is transparency and consistency, not reversing the conclusion. **But one substantive correction the review surfaced is now central to v7:** the compute-parity result attenuates only on the *relative* (log-OR 0.45→0.31) scale; on the *absolute* scale the gain does **not** shrink (aggregate RD ≈ 5 pp > full-sample ≈ 3 pp, *h* ≈ 0.11), because the parity subset excludes the large near-null general-knowledge benchmarks. v6's "≈2 pp, negligible" was wrong in the trivializing direction, and v7 now reports this scale-divergence as a finding in its own right.
- **M4 — direction of the bias.** S040 does not *inflate* the headline; its own log-OR (0.32) is below the pool, so removing it slightly *raises* the estimate. The defect is presentational (an invalid binomial sitting unflagged), not a source of upward bias — handled by foregrounding k = 37 and footnoting S040.
- **V1 — the review's "27 rows" was an over-count.** The true diff is **21 rows / 65 cells**; the edits are proportion-preserving for all but S002 and S023, so effect-size *direction* and the pooled estimate are unaffected. This is documentation/provenance repair, not a change to conclusions.
- **V2 — severity.** The stale figures live in one provenance artifact only; no headline number or conclusion was ever affected. Fixed for auditability, downgraded to minor.
- **m1/m4 — downgraded to non-issue** by the adjudicator (a typo and a display choice with zero inferential cost), but fixed anyway since the cost is trivial.

## Net effect on conclusions

None of the report's substantive conclusions change. The pooled relative effect remains positive, small, and fragile (log-OR 0.45 full / 0.46 accuracy-only; PI crosses zero; I² = 96%; Egger p = .024; "Very Low" certainty). The one *strengthened* message is that the multi-agent advantage attenuates under compute parity on the **relative** scale while the **absolute** gain does not shrink — a scale-dependence that v6 obscured with an unsupported "≈2 pp." v7's edits make every reported number trace to the committed pipeline and make the included-study evidence base, for the first time, auditable.
